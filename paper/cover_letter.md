**To the Editors of Physics Letters B**

C. Peng and J. Zhou
Physics Division, Argonne National Laboratory, Lemont, Illinois 60439, USA

Dear Editors,

We submit for your consideration the manuscript *"Nuclear gluonometry with a
tensor-polarized ⁶Li beam at the Electron-Ion Collider"* for publication in
Physics Letters B.

The manuscript projects the measurement of the double-helicity-flip structure
function Δ(x,Q²) — the one leading-twist structure function of a spin-1 target
that receives no contribution from quarks or from bound spin-½ nucleons, and
therefore a direct probe of glue not inherited from the individual nucleons —
at the Electron-Ion Collider with a transversely tensor-polarized ⁶Li beam.

**Two claims in the manuscript are firsts in the literature, and each was
verified rather than assumed.**

1. **No sensitivity projection of Δ exists for any target.** The observable was
   introduced in 1989 and has never been measured. The only prior experimental
   document is a letter of intent to Jefferson Lab for a ¹⁴N target
   (arXiv:1803.11206), which proposes the measurement but states no reach. The
   projections presented here — inclusive amplitudes resolved at 21–43σ per
   (x,Q²) bin in one EIC year, and the structure function itself returned to
   2–9% in the best bins — are the first numerical sensitivity estimate for
   this observable on any target.

2. **The polarized-nucleus coherent-diffraction programme has not been extended
   past the deuteron.** The colour-glass calculation of Mäntysaari, Salazar,
   Schenke, Shen and Zhao (Phys. Lett. B 858 (2024) 139053) remains the only
   published calculation of polarization-dependent azimuthal coefficients in
   coherent diffraction on a polarized nucleus. We checked every forward
   citation of that paper: none treats a nucleus heavier than the deuteron, and
   the authors' own subsequent work on light nuclei is unpolarized. The
   coherent section of this manuscript is the first treatment of that physics
   for A > 2.

Two further results follow from these and are, to our knowledge, also without
precedent, though we make no citation-sweep claim for them: a quantitative
tagged-yield projection for an intact A = 6 recoil in the EIC far-forward
system, and a null test that the deuteron cannot offer. The second rests on a
property of ⁶Li rather than on a model: its quadrupole moment,
Q = −0.0806(6) fm², is some fifty times smaller than ⁷Li's, so a modulation
flat in t cannot be a nuclear-shape effect, and the shape term itself is
predicted to flip sign relative to the polarized deuteron because
Q(⁶Li) < 0. The nuclear-shape and gluon-transversity mechanisms, degenerate for
the deuteron, therefore separate.

We believe the manuscript suits Physics Letters B on three grounds. It is short
and single-message. It belongs to a genre the journal has published — projection
letters for EIC light-ion measurements, including the polarized-deuteron imaging
proposal cited above. And it is timely: polarized ⁶Li and ⁷Li beams are under
active discussion for the EIC, the manuscript states precisely what the machine
and the far-forward system must provide for the measurement to exist, and those
requirements are being set now.

We note plainly what the projections assume. Structure functions are toy
parameterizations with PDF-grid backends behind the same interface; the
longitudinal-to-transverse ratio is a placeholder whose replacement by the
measured world fit is quoted as a correction to apply; the nuclear PDF set has
no support below Q² = 1.69 GeV², and nothing is claimed on that footing below
that scale. Radiative corrections for tensor observables have not been
calculated anywhere in the literature, and we say so; the unpolarized part is
bounded. The coherent channel rests on a scenario coherent fraction, since no
diffractive prediction exists for a light nucleus, and on a deformation
parameter whose two indicators disagree by a factor three. All of these are
stated in the manuscript's Section 6 rather than in a supplement.

The manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration
by another journal. We have no conflicts of interest to declare. We would be
glad to suggest referees on request.

Yours sincerely,

C. Peng (corresponding author) and J. Zhou
Physics Division, Argonne National Laboratory
