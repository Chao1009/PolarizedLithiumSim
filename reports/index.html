Deep inelastic scattering on polarized lithium: the physics case for ⁶Li and ⁷Li beams at the Electron-Ion Collider

Why lithium — an alkali atom that optical pumping polarizes, two cluster nuclei whose spin sits on the light cluster, ions with deuteron-like spin dynamics; why the physics needs both polarization and a collider; the spin-1 formalism of inclusive DIS; and the four measurements the programme is built around — the tensor structure function b₁, the double-helicity-flip Δ(x,Q²), the polarized EMC effect on the bound proton of ⁷Li, and spectator-tagged DIS — each with what the beam must provide and what one EIC year reaches. Nuclear gluonometry with a tensor-polarized ⁶Li beam at the Electron-Ion Collider: projected inclusive and coherent cos 2φ measurements

The first numerical projection of a Δ(x,Q²) measurement for any target, at the γ-matched beam energies. Inclusive: amplitudes of (0.44–0.95)×10⁻² against δA = (1.4–4.5)×10⁻⁴ per bin in one EIC year (21–44σ), the x dependence discriminating between moment-constrained interpretations, xΔ extracted to 2–9%; at the reconstructed level, with the spin-state ratio and a hadronic-method y from a PYTHIA 8 final state, δÂ = (0.9–3.0)×10⁻⁴ and an unbiased amplitude. Coherent: the deformation and exotic-glue mechanisms, the ⁶Li null test, and the finding that the intact recoil cannot be tagged at IP6 with any published optics — the channel defines a far-forward requirement (a tagging optics or the IR-8 secondary focus), for which one year yields 1.7×10⁷ tagged events; and a lithium tagging optics for IP6 is priced (the horizontal β* raised 46–164× with the pots following, 2.4–6.2×10⁶ tagged events per year at 1/6.8–1/12.8 of the luminosity, 4–10× below an IR-8 secondary focus at equal luminosity) and proposed. 2026-08-28 · reference

The EIC and ePIC for a polarized-lithium programme: beam and detector parameters, their provenance, and the lithium case derived

The specification behind the other reports, every entry carrying its source and its status — design value, requirement, measured, derived here, or unknown. The ion energies follow from the revolution-period constraint (⁶Li at 41 and 99–138 GeV/u, nothing between); the divergence from the Yellow Report's proton tables with a species factor that is unity where the ion is γ-matched and √2 only at the rigidity-capped top (220/380, 180/180, 92/92 μrad); and the far-forward lithium tags evaluated at each optics — at every published one the intact ⁶Li recoil and the ⁶Li α spectator are inside the beam envelope, and a lithium tagging optics (horizontal β* raised 46–164×, pots following, 1/6.8–1/12.8 of the luminosity) recovers a 25–37% coherent and 27–35% α tag: the one machine request this programme makes. The ePIC detector, central and far-forward, fact-checked against the Yellow Report, and fifteen assumptions with owners.

Report 4
2026-08-28 · detector study

Near-beam tagging of the far-forward lithium recoils at ePIC: the aperture, the optics, and whether superconducting nanowires help

The coherent intact-⁶Li tag and the ⁶Li α tag are beam-blind, so the angle at the IP is the only handle and the acceptance is an exponential in its square. Three half-widths compete to set it — the Yellow Report 10σ envelope, the measured ePIC silicon aperture, and the envelope of a lithium tagging optics — and the report prices all three per configuration. At the published optics the machine binds everywhere and no closer approach by any technology recovers a tag; at the tagging optics a layer that follows the 0.12–0.33 mrad envelope is the difference between no tag and a 25–37% tag with seven clean |t| bins per configuration through the full chain, the gain living in a strip 50–180 μrad wide. Superconducting nanowires help with neither role: a dead edge worth 0.005 mrad against a gap set by module tiling and a machine-protection rule, and a one bit that is nearly sufficient for charge identification but a 50% fill factor that is not — while the α + d background separates by hit topology in sensors that exist.
