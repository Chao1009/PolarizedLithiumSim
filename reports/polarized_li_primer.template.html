Deep inelastic scattering on polarized lithium: the physics behind the planned measurements

2026-08-17 · revised 2026-08-25 · companion to the cos 2φ projection report, the reconstruction-chain report and plans/01 (physics case) · written for students and colleagues joining the program · fact-checked against primary sources · modification history in Appendix A

Polarized lithium beams would provide the EIC with spin-polarized nuclei that are light enough for ab initio computation yet complex enough to exhibit genuine nuclear structure. ⁶Li is a spin-1 nucleus well described as a deuteron bound to an inert α core; ⁷Li is its spin-3/2 partner, an α plus a triton. This note develops, starting from the DIS formalism, the four measurements around which the program is built: the tensor structure function b₁, the double-helicity-flip ("exotic glue") structure function Δ, the polarized EMC effect, and spectator-tagged DIS on the embedded deuteron. It begins with the case itself: why lithium is the right element, why the observables exist only with polarization, and which of them a fixed target could deliver and which require the collider.

1 · Why polarized lithium, and why at a collider

The EIC project scope provides polarized electrons, protons and ³He; polarized deuterons are planned as a later addition, and no heavier polarized ion appears in the conceptual design or in the Yellow Report [29,25]. The EPIOS white paper [1] makes the case for polarized ⁶Li and ⁷Li — "a desirable addition" that it lists with ²H and ³He as critical technology — and the ANL source development [51] specifies the beam: an atomic-beam source with optical pumping at 671 nm feeding EBIS charge breeding, with source targets Pz ≥ 0.90 (vector) and Pzz ≥ 0.80 (tensor) at ≥ 10¹³ atoms/s. Four measurements motivate the beam, each addressing a verified gap in the literature: no b₁ prediction or measurement exists for any nucleus beyond the deuteron; no gluon-transversity projection exists for any EIC target; no study of α-tagged DIS on lithium exists; and no lithium-beam luminosity or polarization parameter set has been published. The purpose of the program's simulations is to convert these gaps into figures of merit.

1.1 · Why lithium as a source: an alkali atom with the right nuclei

Lithium is the lightest alkali metal and its atom is hydrogen-like: a single 2s valence electron whose D lines near 671 nm (670.96 and 670.98 nm in vacuum for ⁷Li) fall within the range of commercial single-frequency diode lasers, and whose ground-state hyperfine splittings, 228.2 MHz for ⁶Li and 803.5 MHz for ⁷Li [35], are small enough for one laser with an electro-optic sideband to address both hyperfine levels. Circularly polarized light pumps the atoms into a single magnetic substate, the hyperfine coupling transfers the electron polarization to the nucleus, and radio-frequency transitions between Zeeman sublevels then set any desired combination of Pz and Pzz [51]. The EPIOS paper makes the general point: alkali atoms "are hydrogen-like with a single valence electron and laser cooling of these species has been extensively studied", whereas boron, the other light candidate with a large nuclear spin, has an electronic structure that "presents a challenge for optical pumping" [1]. The low ionization energy of lithium (5.39 eV [35]) eases ionization, and fully stripped Li³⁺ is within reach of the EBIS that is being commissioned to charge-breed the polarized ³He⁺⁺ beam, whose design goal is 2×10¹¹ ions per pulse at ≥ 70% polarization, with the measurement of the polarization transmitted through the EBIS planned for 2026 [34,1].

The nuclei are the other half of the argument. Both isotopes are stable and available (7.6% ⁶Li and 92.4% ⁷Li in natural lithium), and they are the only stable isotopes of one element with J = 1 and J = 3/2: the stable spin-1 nuclei are exactly ²H, ⁶Li and ¹⁴N, and the stable spin-3/2 nuclei below A = 30 are ⁷Li, ⁹Be, ¹¹B, ²¹Ne and ²³Na [36]. One source, one laser system and one injector therefore serve a spin-1 and a spin-3/2 program. The EPIOS assessment of where an exotic-glue discovery is most likely — "spin-1 ⁶Li or ¹⁴N, or spin-3/2 ⁷Li or ²³Na" [1] — reduces, once optical pumping is required, to lithium and sodium, and lithium is the lighter, cluster-structured and ab initio-computable one (§1.2).

Polarized lithium beams also have a forty-year history at low energy, so the source physics is established rather than speculative. The Heidelberg tandem delivered ⁶Li³⁺ beams of up to 150 nA with vector polarization 0.57–0.65 in 1977 [30]; by 1987 its atomic-beam source reached Pz, Pzz ≥ 0.85 and accelerated ⁷Li with an alignment Pzz = 0.69 on target [31]; the optically pumped Florida State source delivered more than 150 nA of ⁶Li³⁺ with Pyy = −1.33 ± 0.02 and Py > 0.74, with no polarization loss in the linac [32]; and polarized ⁶Li, ⁷Li and ²³Na beams served two decades of nuclear-reaction studies [33]. What is new for the EIC is scale and transport: ≥ 10¹³ atoms/s, two to three orders of magnitude beyond these sources, and the preservation of the polarization through charge breeding and acceleration to more than 100 GeV/u [51,1].

In the ring, ⁶Li is the easy case. Its gyromagnetic anomaly, G ≈ −0.18, is as small as the deuteron's (−0.14), so it crosses only 81 depolarizing resonances on the way to top energy, against 63 for the deuteron and 2457 for ³He [28], and, like the deuteron, it is handled with a partial solenoid snake and tune jumps — full Siberian snakes would be impractically long for so small a G [28,1] — so the deuteron techniques [52] carry over. ⁷Li (G ≈ +1.52) crosses 573 resonances and needs partial snakes with jump quadrupoles [1,28]. The rigidity of the hadron ring fixes the top energies at 275 GeV × Z/A: 137.5 GeV/u for ⁶Li and 117.9 GeV/u for ⁷Li. Because ⁶Li has A/Z = 2, it runs at the same per-nucleon energy and magnet settings as the deuteron, so e+d and e+⁶Li can be compared at identical kinematics.

1.2 · Why lithium as a target: the spin sits on a light cluster

The two stable lithium isotopes are the lightest nuclei beyond A = 3 in which a polarized light cluster is bound to an inert core. In ⁶Li the core is an α particle (J = 0, T = 0) and the cluster is deuteron-like, bound by 1.47 MeV against α+d breakup; in ⁷Li the cluster is a triton, bound by 2.47 MeV [36]. Variational Monte Carlo calculations with realistic two- and three-nucleon forces make the picture quantitative: the α–d overlap of the ⁶Li ground state integrates to Nαd = 0.86, of which 0.846 is S-wave and 0.017 D-wave, and the α–t overlap of ⁷Li integrates to Nαt = 1.00 [17]; the no-core shell model with continuum gives an asymptotic D/S ratio of −0.027 for the d+α configuration [38]. The consequence for polarization is what makes the isotopes useful. In a fully polarized ⁶Li the α contributes nothing and the two valence nucleons carry the spin: six-body VMC gives spin-up and spin-down valence numbers of 1.924 and 1.076, a polarization of 0.85 [17,19]; three-body α+n+p models, in which the α+d component accounts for 60–70% of the wave function, give a valence-nucleon polarization above 90% [18]; and the SLAC E155 analysis, which used ⁶LiD as its deuteron target, adopted 87% for the effective deuteron polarization from the same body of calculations [43]. ⁶Li is therefore a polarized deuteron embedded in the field of an α particle — a spin-1 target whose tensor observables can be set against those of the free deuteron to isolate the medium effect. The only other stable spin-1 ground state is ¹⁴N, whose spin is shared among p-shell nucleons, which has no comparable cluster simplicity, and which lies beyond the reach of exact quantum Monte Carlo (A ≤ 12) [40].

In ⁷Li the spin is carried by the unpaired proton of the triton cluster, the neutrons being paired [39]. VMC gives spin-up and spin-down proton numbers of 1.934 and 1.066 and neutron numbers of 1.981 and 2.019 [17], i.e. Pp = 0.87 and Pn = −0.04 — quoted as 0.866 and −0.037 in the JLab proposal [45] and used in §4.3. The shell model already gives Pp = 13/15; the microscopic calculations confirm the proton value and show the neutrons almost unpolarized [19,14]. Polarized ⁷Li is thus to the proton what polarized ³He is to the neutron, with the property, decisive for the polarized EMC effect, that the proton sits in a nucleus of normal density. Cloët, Bentz and Thomas state the selection criteria: the nucleus must have |Pn| ≪ |Pp|, and it must be light, because g₁ᴬ is carried by the valence nucleons and is suppressed relative to F₂ᴬ by roughly 1/A; their candidates are ¹¹B, ¹⁵N, ²⁷Al and ⁷Li, "where the nuclear polarization is largely dominated by the valence proton" [14], and the gluon-spin study names ⁷Li "the most promising case for experimental investigation" [16].

Two further properties matter. The cluster structure makes the nuclei computable: Green's-function Monte Carlo with the AV18+IL7 Hamiltonian reproduces the binding energies of both isotopes (−31.93 vs −31.99 MeV for ⁶Li, −39.15 vs −39.25 MeV for ⁷Li), their charge radii to within 3%, and, once two-body currents are included, their magnetic moments (0.835 vs 0.822 μN; 3.24 vs 3.256 μN) [40,39], so the nuclear corrections entering every extraction — effective polarizations, momentum distributions, spectral functions — carry controlled uncertainties rather than model spread. The quadrupole moment of ⁶Li is the one exception: the measured Q = −0.0806 fm², against +0.286 fm² for the free deuteron [36], arises from a delicate cancellation between the deuteron's own quadrupole moment and the D-wave part of the α–d relative motion [19,37], and theory reproduces only its smallness. That smallness is itself an asset. ⁶Li is nearly spherical, so tensor effects of geometric origin (the deformation term of the coherent channel, §4.2) are bounded and a tensor signal must be dynamical in origin; ⁷Li, whose p-shell triton orbit gives Q = −4.00 fm² [36], is the deformed counterpart. Finally, the two isotopes share one source, one injector and one detector: the α+d versus α+t comparison changes only the cluster, the cleanest controlled variation the program has.

1.3 · Why polarization is indispensable

An unpolarized lithium beam would be one more light ion. Its inclusive cross section is carried by F₁ and F₂ alone — for an unpolarized target the spin-1 cross section has exactly the spin-½ form [2] — and it would add an A = 6, 7 point to the EMC and shadowing curves that ⁴He and ¹²C already map. Every item of the program is instead a spin observable, and the two flagship items need the tensor moment specifically. b₁ is defined as the difference between the m = 0 and m = ±1 quark distributions; averaging over m erases it, and it can be reached only with tensor polarization — which is why the only existing measurement is HERMES, with the only tensor-polarized target ever used in DIS [5]. Δ is generated by gluon transversity, an interference between gluon helicity states that requires a target spin of at least 1 [3], and its cos 2φ signature needs a transverse alignment axis to define the azimuth: the inclusive cross section of an unpolarized target has no azimuthal dependence at all. The polarized EMC effect is by construction a statement about g₁, and the gluon-spin EMC effect enters only through the Q² evolution of g₁ [16]. In tagged DIS the spectator momentum selects S- versus D-wave configurations of the polarized pair, removing depolarization at small pT and producing tensor asymmetries of order unity near 300 MeV/c [41]: the spin is the signal. Since the EIC project polarizes only protons and ³He, with deuterons to follow [29], polarized lithium would be the first polarized ion at a collider with a nuclear core — the first place where the spin structure of a nucleus, rather than of a nucleon, is studied at collider kinematics. Tensor polarization is the scarcer resource (source targets Pzz ≥ 0.80 against Pz ≥ 0.90 [51], with ring transport still to be established), and both b₁ and Δ depend on it.

1.4 · Fixed target or collider?

Polarized lithium has existed for decades as a fixed target, but only in the solid state. Dynamically polarized ⁶LiD was the deuteron target of SLAC E155 and of COMPASS, ⁷LiH has been polarized at Saclay (47% for ⁷Li, 56% for the protons) [44], and dynamically polarized ⁷LiD (with ⁶LiH or NH₃ as the proton reference) is the material of the approved CLAS12 polarized-EMC experiment [43,42,45]. In these compounds the lithium polarizes along with the hydrogen isotope because all spin species share one spin temperature: COMPASS reached deuteron polarizations of +54%/−47%, and the equal-spin-temperature relation gives 51 ± 3% for ⁶Li and 92 ± 4% for the ⁷Li admixture [42]; E155 measured the ⁶Li polarization at 97% of the deuteron's [43]; in ⁷LiD at 5 T the CLAS12 proposal expects 65–80% for ⁷Li [45]. Three limits are structural. (i) Dilution: only a fraction f ≈ 0.35–0.45 of the nucleons in ⁶LiD are polarizable once the helium coolant is counted — better than 0.22 for ND₃ and 0.15 for NH₃, but far from the 1.0 of a stored beam [42,43]. (ii) Tensor polarization: in thermal equilibrium the spin-1 tensor moment follows from the vector one, Pzz = 2 − √(4 − 3Pz²), so Pz = 0.5 gives Pzz = 0.20; radio-frequency "hole burning" has raised it to 29–37% in deuterated butanol at the cost of the vector polarization, and the JLab b₁ experiment is conditionally approved on demonstrating 30% [8,46]. An atomic-beam source prepares the tensor moment directly: HERMES ran its gas target at Pzz = +0.89 and −1.65 in the pure-tensor states, "a combination which is not possible for solid-state polarized targets" [5], and the lithium source targets Pzz ≥ 0.80 [51]. (iii) Spectator loss: a spectator α of 100–300 MeV/c carries only 1.3–12 MeV in the target rest frame and stops within 10–250 μm of lithium deuteride (NIST ASTAR ranges [49]) — inside target cells 2–5 cm long at JLab and 30–60 cm long at COMPASS — and a coherent ⁶Li recoil at |t| = 0.05 GeV² has only 4.5 MeV. The JLab recoil detectors that do tag slow spectators, BoNuS (protons from 70 MeV/c) and ALERT (α from 230 MeV/c), work with unpolarized gas at a few atmospheres [47,48]; polarized-lithium targets outside dynamically polarized solids are essentially unknown — a 1975 polarized-⁶Li target for a low-energy reaction study [50] is the one instance on record — and none has been used in lepton scattering.

The collider removes each limit at once. The beam is pure lithium with its polarization prepared atom by atom and measured in the ring; the spectator α or triton carries the beam momentum per nucleon and emerges at a few milliradians into the Roman Pots, off-momentum detectors, B0 and zero-degree calorimeter, with ~10% transverse-momentum resolution at 100 MeV/c [22]; the intact coherent recoil can be tagged down to pT ≈ 0 with the IR-8 secondary focus [27]; and the centre-of-mass energy — √sNN up to 99 GeV for e(18 GeV) × ⁶Li(137.5 GeV/u) — opens x down to ~10⁻⁴ at Q² ≥ 1 GeV², where JLab at 11 GeV stops at x ≈ 0.06 and HERMES stopped at 0.01 [45,5]. That is the region where gluons dominate, where HERMES saw its large b₁, and where coherence holds. What stays with the fixed target is luminosity: 10³⁵ cm⁻² s⁻¹ with dynamically polarized targets (CLAS12 at 10–15 nA, Hall C at 115 nA, with the material annealed every 2–3 days) [45,8] against an electron–nucleon luminosity of 10³³–10³⁴ cm⁻² s⁻¹ at the EIC [29,25]. The high-x polarized EMC effect and the high-x b₁ of the deuteron are therefore rightly JLab measurements, and the EIC program concentrates on what the table summarizes: the low-x and high-Q² regions, the tensor observables at full polarization, and everything that needs a detected spectator or recoil.

1.5 · What each observable requires of the beam, and what lithium alone provides

The requirements below are the program's own, from the fast simulation and the polligen pseudo-experiments (§5), stated for the placeholder in-ring polarizations Pz = 0.7 and Pzz = 0.6, Pe = 0.7, and 10 fb⁻¹/u. Every statistical error scales as 1/(P√L), so a lower polarization is paid for in luminosity squared: halving Pzz costs a factor four in running time. Two machine facts shape the list. The stable spin direction in the arcs is vertical, so transverse polarization at the interaction point is native while longitudinal polarization needs spin rotators, whose lithium settings are not yet defined [29,1]; and tensor fills need m = 0 bunches prepared by the source radio-frequency transitions, whose survival through acceleration and bunch-by-bunch patterning are open questions [51]. The observables also demand different spin configurations and therefore share the luminosity; the reach quoted assumes each receives the full 10 fb⁻¹/u in its own configuration. What lithium alone provides is clearest species by species. Of the ions the EIC can polarize, only the deuteron and the lithium isotopes have J ≥ 1, so b₁ and Δ do not exist for protons or ³He at all; among J ≥ 1 nuclei ⁶Li is the only A > 2 case, and the lattice indication that the deuteron's exotic glue is suppressed [11] is what makes a bound nucleus, rather than the deuteron, the likelier place for a discovery [1]. ⁷Li is the only bound polarized proton within reach of any collider; ⁶Li and the deuteron supply the isoscalar companions, so the three together separate the u- and d-quark spin modification — an inference of this program, not yet in the literature. For tagging, ⁷Li gives the only light-ion spectator that is a spin-0, tightly bound nucleus landing in the Roman-Pot window today, leaving a struck A = 3 cluster. In the coherent channel the deuteron's deformation term dominates (Q = +0.286 fm²), ⁷Li is the deformed reference (Q = −4.00 fm²), and ⁶Li (Q = −0.081 fm²) is the deformation null in which a flat-in-t modulation can only be exotic glue.

What an unpolarized lithium beam already gives. Three things, none of them the driver. First, nuclear parton distributions at A = 6 and 7: the high-x EMC ratios of ⁶Li and ⁷Li are in the target list of JLab E12-10-008 [54], motivated by the E03-103 finding that the ⁹Be EMC effect follows the local density of its α clusters rather than its average density [53] — for ⁶Li and ⁷Li the local-density picture predicts an EMC effect well below the other models [54] — and the EIC adds the low-x shadowing region. Second, cluster virtuality: the α-tagged F₂ of the triton (⁷Li) or deuteron (⁶Li) cluster versus spectator momentum measures the α–t and α–d momentum distributions that VMC predicts (Nαt = 1.00 in a relative p-wave, Nαd = 0.86) [17], the collider counterpart of the JLab tagged-EMC program [48]. Third, coherent diffraction on an α-clustered nucleus, whose t-distribution images the gluon density of the cluster, as studied for ⁷Li at IR-8 [27]. Polarization turns the same cluster structure from an object of study into a tool: b₁, Δ, g₁ᴬ and the tagged tensor asymmetries all vanish or are undefined without it (§1.3).

3 · Spin-1 targets

A spin-½ target contributes two spin structure functions, g₁ and g₂. The principal observable is g₁, the helicity-weighted quark distribution, measured through the double-spin asymmetry A∥ ≈ D·g₁/F₁ (D is a kinematic depolarization factor) with both beams longitudinally polarized and the electron helicity reversed bunch by bunch.

A spin-1 target carries additional structure. Its spin state along a chosen axis is specified by three populations p₊, p₀, p₋ of the projections m = +1, 0, −1, and two moments characterize the fill:

Pz is the familiar vector polarization. Pzz, the tensor or alignment polarization, is specific to spin ≥ 1: it distinguishes spin along ±z from spin in the transverse plane (m = 0), and it is nonzero even when equal numbers point up and down. An unpolarized beam has populations (⅓, ⅓, ⅓) and both moments vanish; a pure m = 0 beam has Pz = 0 but Pzz = −2.

Hoodbhoy, Jaffe, and Manohar showed [2] that a spin-1 target has, beyond F₁, F₂, g₁, g₂, four tensor-polarized structure functions b₁–b₄, and Jaffe and Manohar identified one more, the double-helicity-flip function Δ [3]. At leading twist the tensor sector reduces to b₁ (with b₂ = 2x b₁ as its Callan–Gross partner) and Δ. The weights (1, −2, 1) organize all tensor observables: the m = ±1 states enter together and against m = 0, precisely the combination measured by Pzz. Averaging the formula over a fill with populations pm replaces every cm by a factor Pzz. Two experimental handles follow. With the spin axis longitudinal, the rate difference between fills gives Azz ∝ b₁; with the spin axis transverse (θm = 90°), the φ distribution of the scattered electron acquires a cos 2φ modulation proportional to Δ. Both require tensor polarization; neither requires polarized electrons.

⁷Li, with spin 3/2, has four populations and three moments (vector, tensor/quadrupole, and octupole). Its rank-0 and rank-1 sectors are exact analogs of the spin-½ case, and host the polarized EMC measurement; its rank-2 sector is the formal analog of the spin-1 tensor sector, for which no structure-function basis has been published — an open theory item the program tracks.

4 · The measurement program

4.1 · The tensor structure function b₁

In parton language, b₁ compares quark distributions in an aligned versus a transverse nucleus [2]:

where q(m) is the quark distribution in a target with spin projection m. For a collection of free spin-½ nucleons, or for a pure S-wave bound state, all m states are equivalent and b₁ = 0. A nonzero b₁ therefore measures directly how binding modifies the partonic content: D-wave admixture, pion exchange, hidden-color components, or a tensor-polarized sea (which would violate the Close–Kumano sum rule [4]).

The only existing measurement is HERMES on the deuteron [5]: Azz up to 2×10⁻² over 0.01 < x < 0.45 and b₁(x = 0.012) = 0.112 ± 0.055 ± 0.028, unexpectedly large at low x, with a zero crossing near x ≈ 0.2 and a sum-rule integral 2σ from zero. Standard convolution calculations predict |b₁| < 10⁻³ at x ≳ 0.2, an order of magnitude below the data [6], whereas a model with pions and a hidden-color probability of only 0.15% reproduces it [7]. The tension is unresolved; JLab E12-13-011 will remeasure the region 0.16 < x < 0.49 with a solid ND₃ target [8], and the EIC adds the low-x region, where HERMES observed the large signal, together with the Q² lever arm.

Measurement procedure. The beam is delivered in fills of different tensor polarization ("tensor thirds"). With the estimator (N₊ + N₋ − 2N₀) over matched luminosities, all m-independent physics cancels, and the statistical uncertainty on Azz is √(2/N)/Pzz. ⁶Li would provide the first b₁ of any A > 2 nucleus, for which no prediction exists, and the difference between ⁶Li and the free deuteron isolates the effect of embedding in the α field. One systematic is tracked from the outset: at collider kinematics a clean b₁ extraction favors orienting the spin axis along the momentum transfer q rather than along the beam [26].

4.2 · Δ(x,Q²): exotic glue

The cos 2φ term in the master formula corresponds to the virtual photon flipping its helicity by two units between absorption and emission in the forward Compton amplitude. A spin-½ particle cannot absorb two units of helicity, so neither quarks nor bound nucleons generate Δ at leading twist; gluons can. Δ is the structure function that the gluon transversity distribution, an interference of gluon helicity states existing only in targets with J ≥ 1, generates at order αs through a photon–gluon convolution [3,9]. A nonzero Δ in a nucleus is therefore direct evidence for glue not inherited from individual nucleons, hence the name "exotic glue". It has not been measured for any target; the one prior experimental document is a JLab letter of intent [13].

The measurement uses unpolarized electrons on a tensor-polarized nucleus whose alignment axis lies in the transverse plane, and fits the azimuthal distribution about that axis. Averaged over the fill, the modulation amplitude and its inversion back to Δ are

The expected size spans per-mille to percent. The only absolute normalizations available are moment estimates: a bag-model calculation by Sather and Schmidt for the spin-3/2 Δ⁺⁺ baryon, ∫x Δ dx = −0.012 αs [9], and lattice-QCD calculations by Detmold and Shanahan of gluon transversity in the φ meson (clearly nonzero, albeit at an unphysical pion mass) [10], with NPLQCD finding the analogous matrix element roughly ten times suppressed in the deuteron [11]. Percent-level asymmetries appear only in proton–deuteron Drell–Yan under the maximal assumption ΔTg = Δg, described by its authors as optimistic [12]. No calculation exists for ⁶Li or any A > 2 nucleus, and the literature brackets the nuclear Δ in both directions: suppressed, as in the lattice deuteron, or enhanced by binding in analogy with the EMC effect [1]. The projection report transfers the bag moment to ⁶Li as a labeled scenario, shows that one EIC year measures the amplitude at ≥ 20σ per bin, and details the caveats.

The coherent channel. The same modulation can be measured in coherent diffraction, e ⁶Li → e′ X ⁶Li(g.s.), in which the nucleus remains in its ground state and recoils intact. There the cos 2φ modulation mixes two mechanisms: the nuclear shape, in which a deformed gluon density modulates the diffractive slope (the mechanism computed for polarized deuterons in the only existing calculation of this kind [23]), and exotic glue proper, which persists at small x where it connects to the elliptic-gluon program [24]. ⁶Li discriminates between the two because its quadrupole moment is anomalously small (Q = −0.0806 fm², roughly 50 times smaller than that of ⁷Li): the shape term is bounded and linear in t, so a flat-in-t modulation cannot be a shape effect. Detection of the intact recoil is itself nontrivial: the nucleus keeps the beam rigidity, as any coherent recoil does, and emerges within ~2 mrad of the beam, visible only in the Roman-Pot transverse-momentum tail at IP6 or at the secondary focus of a second interaction region [25,27]; the companion report treats this in detail.

4.3 · The polarized EMC effect: g₁ of a bound proton

The EMC effect — the ~10–20% suppression of F₂ per nucleon in a nucleus at 0.3 ≲ x ≲ 0.7 — has been measured for forty years without a settled explanation. Its spin analog has never been measured at all: does the spin structure of a bound nucleon change more, less, or the same as its momentum structure? The observable is the polarized EMC ratio

where Pp, Pn are the effective nucleon polarizations from nuclear structure. ⁷Li is well suited: the variational Monte Carlo spin-projected nucleon numbers of Wiringa et al. give Pp = +0.87 and Pn = −0.04 (0.866 and −0.037 as quoted in the JLab proposal) [17,45], so to a good approximation polarized ⁷Li is one polarized proton plus six spectators, with computable corrections. Two classes of models make sharply different predictions: Cloët–Bentz–Thomas (mean-field, NJL) obtain a polarized EMC effect about twice the unpolarized one [14], whereas Tronchin–Matevosyan–Thomas obtain equal effects [15]. A measurement at the 5% level discriminates between them, and the two map onto the competing mean-field and short-range-correlation pictures of the EMC effect itself. A handle unique to the EIC is the Q² evolution of g₁, driven by the polarized gluon distribution; the predicted gluon-spin EMC effect is larger still, with ⁷Li identified as the most promising case [16]. JLab E12-14-001 will measure ⁷Li at x > 0.06 [45]; the EIC extends the coverage to x < 0.06 and adds the Q² arm, in collider kinematics free of target-fragment absorption.

For this observable both beams are polarized longitudinally and the electron helicity flips bunch-by-bunch; the error scales as 1/(PePz√N), so it uses the vector polarization the source delivers most readily.

4.4 · Spectator-tagged DIS

The cluster description of ⁶Li is quantitative (§1.2): the α is inert, and the deuteron-like pair carries the nuclear spin with a polarization of 0.85–0.9 in six- and three-body calculations [17,18,19], the 0.87 adopted by E155 [43] being the value the program uses. When the α spectator is detected (it carries beam velocity into the far-forward spectrometers), the struck object is identified as the deuteron cluster, and the ⁶Li beam acts as a source of polarized deuterons at controlled virtuality. The formal framework is spectator tagging (Cosyn–Weiss [20]): the spectator momentum is measured and extrapolated in its transverse component to the free-nucleon (here free-cluster) pole, recovering free-particle structure with binding effects removed by construction rather than modeled. Tagged tensor asymmetries are predicted to reach O(1) at spectator momenta of ~300 MeV/c, where the D-wave dominates [41] — far larger than the inclusive asymmetries above. The benchmark for the method is the e+³He double-tagging study of Friščić et al. [21], in which tagging improved on effective-polarization unfolding by a factor of 2–3, and by a factor of 10 at low x.

Far-forward routing determines where each isotope's tag fragment is detected, and it follows from magnetic rigidity alone: a beam-velocity fragment with mass-to-charge ratio A/Z bends in the hadron optics according to R = (A/Z)frag/(A/Z)beam.

5 · Rates and statistical precision

All projections use luminosity per nucleon, with 10 fb⁻¹/u — 30 weeks of operation at 10³³ cm⁻² s⁻¹ with 60% efficiency in the Yellow Report's accounting [25], rounded here to "one EIC year" — and 100 fb⁻¹/u for the ten-year program, and in-ring polarization placeholders Pz = 0.7 and Pzz = 0.6 (ring transport from the source values is an open accelerator question). The factor √2 in the tensor uncertainties follows from ⟨cos²2φ⟩ = ½ (equivalently, from the three-fill estimator variance); the polarization appears in the denominator because the fill dilutes the physical asymmetry. These formulas, and their behavior under realistic φ-acceptance holes (where a binned fit replaces the naive moment), are validated end to end by pseudo-experiments with the polligen event generator. Tensor observables enjoy a structural advantage in systematics when the m = ±1-rich and m = 0-rich states are measured with the same detector and compared bin by bin: in their spin-state ratio the φ acceptance cancels and relative-luminosity errors enter only as a constant (a single-state fit, by contrast, measures the detector's own cos 2φ acceptance harmonic divided by Pzz), and the two-state pattern P₊ = +0.6, P₀ = −1.2 is 1.5× more precise than a single state at the same source purity — provided the states alternate bunch by bunch, since a 10⁻³ difference of the acceptance harmonic between them fakes half the signal (reconstruction-chain report §3). What remains is the polarimetry scale (δPzz/Pzz ≈ 3%) and radiative corrections, which for tensor observables have not yet been calculated.
