Abstract. The double-helicity-flip structure function Δ(x,Q²) of a spin-1 target is generated at leading twist by gluons alone and has never been measured. We project its measurement at the Electron-Ion Collider with a transversely tensor-polarized ⁶Li beam, in inclusive deep inelastic scattering, where only the scattered electron is detected, and in coherent diffraction with the ⁶Li recoiling intact. With the leading-order bag-model moment of Sather and Schmidt ported to ⁶Li as a labelled scenario, the inclusive cos 2φ amplitudes at the four most sensitive (x, Q²) bins, x = 0.011–0.14 and Q² = 1.1–14 GeV², are (0.44–0.95)×10⁻² at Pzz = 0.6, against single-state truth-level statistical errors of (1.4–4.5)×10⁻⁴ in one EIC year (10 fb⁻¹ per nucleon): a 21–43σ measurement per bin whose x dependence discriminates between moment-constrained interpretations, and an extraction of xΔ(x,Q²) itself to 2–9% in the best bins. At the reconstructed level, with the spin-state-sorted ratio of m = ±1-rich and m = 0-rich bunches and a hadronic-method y from a PYTHIA 8 final state, the errors are (0.9–3.0)×10⁻⁴ and the fit closes on the response-folded truth. The coherent channel carries the same modulation with two separable mechanisms — a nuclear-shape term anchored on the polarized-deuteron colour-glass calculation, predicted to flip sign for ⁶Li, and a flat exotic-glue term — and ⁶Li's anomalously small quadrupole moment makes it a null test. Its intact recoil keeps the beam rigidity and, with the Yellow Report's beam divergences, cannot be tagged at IP6 with any published optics; the channel defines a far-forward requirement — a dedicated tagging optics or the second interaction region's secondary focus — and for a reference tag with a 0.20 GeV near-beam envelope at the high-acceptance luminosity — which no IP6 optics provides — one year would yield 1.7×10⁷ events and a 5σ sensitivity to an amplitude of 0.9% per unit Pzz, against a shape term of 6% and an exotic-glue term of 0.3–1% on the same footing. A tagging optics at IP6 — the horizontal β* raised 46–164× with the pots following the envelope, the vertical plane untouched — recovers a 25–37% tag at 1/6.8–1/12.8 of the luminosity: 2.4–6.1×10⁶ tagged events per year, an 8–11σ measurement of the shape term per year and 5σ on a 1% exotic-glue term in 3–6 years, both on the counting floor of the best (x, Q²) super-bin alone (the seven-|t|-bin fit of the whole tagged sample measures the flat term to δae = 0.07–0.12% per unit Pzz per year), 4–10× below what a second interaction region's secondary focus would deliver at the same luminosity.

1 · Introduction

A spin-1 target can absorb two units of photon helicity in the forward Compton amplitude. The structure function that measures this, Δ(x,Q²), receives no leading-twist contribution from quarks or from bound spin-½ nucleons, so in a nucleus it is direct evidence for glue that is not inherited from the individual nucleons — "exotic glue" in the language of Jaffe and Manohar's nuclear gluonometry [1,2]. It has never been measured for any target; the one prior experimental document is a JLab letter of intent for a ¹⁴N target, without a sensitivity projection [3]. The only absolute normalizations available are moment estimates: a bag-model calculation for the spin-3/2 Δ⁺⁺ baryon [4], maximal-transversity scenarios for proton–deuteron Drell–Yan [5], and lattice-QCD matrix elements for the φ meson [6] and the deuteron [7]. The EPIOS white paper on polarized ion beams at the EIC names spin-1 ⁶Li among the nuclei where a discovery is most likely [8], and the companion physics case [9] argues why ⁶Li — a polarized deuteron bound to an inert α particle, with A/Z = 2 and deuteron-like spin dynamics in the ring — is the practical choice.

This paper presents the first numerical projection of a Δ measurement for any target. Two channels are considered. In inclusive DIS on a transversely tensor-polarized ⁶Li beam the scattered electron's azimuth about the alignment axis acquires a cos 2φ modulation proportional to Δ. In coherent diffraction, e ⁶Li → e′ X ⁶Li(g.s.), the nucleus stays in its ground state and the modulation of the tagged yield mixes the gluonic term with a nuclear-shape term that the polarized-deuteron calculation of Mäntysaari et al. [10] predicts — and ⁶Li, with a quadrupole moment fifty times smaller than ⁷Li's, separates the two. Section 2 states the observable, Section 3 the simulation framework, Section 4 the Δ model, Section 5 the inclusive projections at truth and reconstructed level, Section 6 the coherent channel with the far-forward requirement it imposes, Section 7 the systematics and assumptions.

The sign is the one that helps: for a positive b₁ the leakage opposes the amplitude of a negative Δ rather than faking one, and being proportional to b₁ it is subtractable with the Azz the same fills measure.

The size of Δ is unknown to within an order of magnitude. The bag-model moment of Sather and Schmidt, ∫x Δ dx = −0.012 αs with its Q² evolution but no x dependence [4], puts the asymmetry at the per-mille level; the explicit αs(Q²) is the leading-order Wilson coefficient of the photon–gluon box, so the O(αs) suppression relative to F₁ is model-independent while −0.012 is the bag estimate. Lattice QCD adds two results, both at unphysical pion masses: the bare φ-meson matrix element A₂ = 0.23(2)(5) at mπ = 450 MeV [6], which converts to ∫x Δ|φ ≈ −0.006 αs (our conversion, unrenormalized), and the NPLQCD deuteron δG moment of −0.010(3), bare at mπ ≈ 806 MeV [7] — a transversity-to-momentum ratio roughly ten times below the φ's, implying the opposite sign for the deuteron's Δ moment. Percent-level asymmetries arise only in the maximal-transversity (ΔTg = Δg) Drell–Yan scenarios their authors call optimistic [5]. Nothing exists for ⁶Li or any A > 2 nucleus beyond a ΔΔ-isobar estimate for the deuteron [12], so the literature brackets the nuclear Δ in both directions: suppressed, as in the NPLQCD deuteron, or enhanced by binding in analogy with the EMC effect [8]. The projections therefore carry the moment-constrained model of §4 together with the scenario band Δ/F₁ ∈ 10⁻³–10⁻².

3 · Simulation framework

3.1 · Beams, polarization and luminosity

EIC ions are γ-matched to the proton rather than rigidity-scaled, because the two rings must share a revolution period, so the ⁶Li menu is 40.8 GeV/u and 99.5–137.5 GeV/u, the top value being the rigidity cap 275 GeV × Z/A [8,13]. The projections are made at the mid configuration, e(10 GeV) × ⁶Li(99.5 GeV/u), √sNN = 63 GeV, with the low (5 × 40.8) and top (18 × 137.5) configurations used where the hadronic reconstruction or the far-forward acceptance depends on the energy. The in-ring tensor polarization is a placeholder Pzz = 0.6 (the ANL source targets Pzz ≥ 0.8 [9]; transport to the ring is an open accelerator question, and every statistical error scales as 1/Pzz), the alignment axis is the vertical — the stable spin direction of the arcs, assumed to reach the interaction point unrotated, which is one of the three demands §8 makes on the machine — and the electrons are unpolarized. Luminosity is quoted per nucleon: 10 fb⁻¹/u, "one EIC year" in the Yellow Report's accounting (30 weeks at 10³³ cm⁻² s⁻¹ with 60% efficiency [13]), and 100 fb⁻¹/u for the ten-year programme, all of it in the transverse-tensor fill. No lithium luminosity has been published; the yields scale linearly with whatever the machine delivers. The inclusive and coherent measurements want different far-forward optics — the Yellow Report divergence for the first, the tagging optics of §6.1 at 1/6.8 to 1/12.8 of the luminosity for the second — and each projection assumes the full luminosity of the year in its own, so the two reaches are alternatives rather than a sum; the share the run plan actually gives each is a programme decision, priced in plans/07 WP2, and every statistical error below scales as one over the square root of it, while a luminosity quoted as a reach in fb⁻¹/u stands where it is and the years needed to accumulate it grow as one over the share. What does not follow that law is anything in an error bar that is not statistics: the full one-year Δ̂ bars of Report 2 [16] carry the spread over unfolding priors, which no luminosity shrinks, and it has to be separated out before a share is applied.

3.2 · Generator and structure functions

No public generator produces polarized e+A events for A > 1: BeAGLE is unpolarized, DJANGOH and PEPSI are nucleon-level and longitudinal-only. The generator used here, polligen, implements the missing component — the spin-density matrix ⊗ structure-function master formula with spin-correlated sampling — and takes the hadronic final state from a PYTHIA 8 production of 8 M events over the three beam configurations. It is validated against the analytic asymmetry formulas and by pseudo-experiments, including unbiased cos 2φ recovery under φ-acceptance holes; a full-luminosity pseudo-experiment draws each φ-bin count from a Poisson distribution with the exact expected yield, which is statistically identical to event-level sampling.

Structure functions are toy parameterizations with PDF-grid backends (CT18NLO, EPPS21, NNPDFpol11) behind one interface, so rates hold to a factor ≈ 1.5 (the toy F₂ is within ±37% of CT18 over the acceptance) and asymmetry shapes are scenarios by construction. R = σL/σT is a placeholder of the right magnitude, 0.14–0.18 at the four bins of §3.4, in every projection below; the published SLAC/E143 R1998 world fit [14], all three of its functional forms, sits behind the same hook, and switching to it moves Δ/F₁ by +18, +18, +8 and −4% at the four bins, with a further 0.5–6% from the spread of the fit forms — a correction to apply rather than a band, and the systematic on every Δ quoted below until it is.

3.3 · Estimators

The figures use a single spin state: a binned least-squares fit of N(φ′) = C(1 + A cos 2φ′) rather than the 2⟨cos 2φ′⟩ moment, which realistic φ holes bias by more than the signal while the fit stays unbiased, with δA = √(2/N)/Pzz validated by pseudo-experiments and no cos φ′ term, since the m-symmetric tensor fills set it to zero by construction. The azimuth φ′ is the covariant one built from the four-vectors [15], equal to φe − φS for a massless target and differing from it by γ²/4 radians for a massive one — 1.4 mrad at the four bins of §3.4, 5.9 mrad at the low configuration's own spots [16] — and the 25 mrad crossing angle changes only odd harmonics of the electron azimuth because the ePIC axis is the electron beam.

The measurement itself cannot use a single state: the fit then also measures the detector's own cos 2φ′ acceptance harmonic divided by Pzz, and a 3% harmonic aligned with the vertical spin axis fakes an amplitude of 0.05, five to eleven times the signal. The estimator is therefore the spin-state-sorted ratio of m = ±1-rich (Pzz = +0.6) and m = 0-rich (−1.2) bunches. It cancels any acceptance common to both states bin by bin, turns relative-luminosity errors into a constant, and gives, with N the total of both states, δÂ = 2√(2/N)/(P₊ − P₀) = 0.67× the single-state error if the m = 0-rich fill reaches |Pzz| = 1.2. The cancellation of the acceptance, however, holds only for an acceptance common to both states: for two fills the bias is (ε₂⁺ − ε₂⁰)/(P₊ − P₀) at any luminosity split, so a 10⁻³ difference of the cos 2φ′ harmonic fakes 5.6×10⁻⁴, 6–13% of the projected amplitudes and 2–6 one-year statistical errors. Bunch-by-bunch alternation, or 10⁻⁴ stability of the harmonic between fills, is thus a requirement of the measurement rather than a refinement; with it, a relative-luminosity error between the states enters only as a constant.

3.4 · Acceptance and binning

Rates use per-nucleon luminosity, F₂ᴬ/A, and scattered-electron cuts |η| ≤ 3.5, E′ ≥ 0.5 GeV, 0.01 ≤ y ≤ 0.95 and W² ≥ 10 GeV², which accept 5.2×10⁹ events in one year (Figure 1). The four displayed (x, Q²) super-bins are selected by statistical significance within Q² bands — an unconstrained ranking concentrates at the lowest Q², whereas the Q² lever arm provides the consistency check against power-suppressed contributions — and each merges 3×3 cells of the 40×30 analysis grid (3×2 for the two Q² = 1.14 GeV² bins, clipped at the grid's Q² = 1 GeV² edge). They sit at (x, Q²) = (0.028, 1.14), (0.011, 1.14), (0.071, 3.13) and (0.141, 14.3 GeV²), i.e. at y = 0.010, 0.025, 0.011 and 0.025: the amplitude grows as (1 − y)/y², so the sensitivity lives at the lowest y the detector can reconstruct, which is what makes the kinematic reconstruction of §5.2 the central detector question.

It is a counting fraction and not a polarization: the cluster picture that fixes the vector polarization of that same pair — 0.811 of the ⁶Li spin, carried by g₁(⁶Li) since 2026-08-29 — is a rank-1 quantity, while Δ, like b₁, is rank 2, where the α–d wave function transfers 0.92 rather than 0.87 (Report 0, Table 3), so no upside on this factor follows from it; whether Δ should carry that rank-2 transfer as b₁ does — 1/3 → 0.307, an 8% reduction — is left open in the Δ-model registry, and no projection here assumes it. The constraint is imposed at the rate-weighted ⟨Q²⟩ = 4.47 GeV² of the accepted phase space, and because F₁ evolves the realized moment varies by a few per cent across the plotted Q² slices; αs comes from the CT18NLO table. Model B imposes the same constraint without the F₁ factor and yields amplitudes a factor 3–56 lower at the four bins (20–56 in the Q² = 1.14 GeV² bins, 3–8 at the higher Q²); the A-versus-B spread is the dominant ansatz systematic, and both curves appear on every plot. Porting a single-hadron bag moment to a per-nucleon nuclear structure function has no precedent, so normalization and x dependence are both scenario choices, bracketed by model B from below and possible nuclear enhancement from above.

5 · Inclusive projections

5.1 · Truth level

Figure 2 shows the φ′ pseudo-data in the four super-bins with one-year statistics and the extracted amplitude versus x along the Q² = 1.14 GeV² slice. The injected amplitudes are 7.4, 4.4, 9.5 and 9.5 ×10⁻³ against one-year errors of 1.7, 1.4, 2.7 and 4.5 ×10⁻⁴ — 43, 31, 35 and 21σ — and ten-year errors of 0.55, 0.45, 0.87 and 1.4 ×10⁻⁴; the fitted values agree with the injected ones within 1.5 standard errors. 5.3 · Bin centering

Correcting the reconstructed amplitude to a point value of Δ with the model's own K is circular. Because the amplitude is exactly linear in Δ, the response can instead be applied as a linear operator and a Δ(x) shape fitted through it per Q² slice on a bounded grid. Correcting the same pseudo-data with either of two deliberately wrong priors, the model dependence of K at the four super-bins falls from (−4.2, +8.0, −5.6, +4.9)% to (−1.5, −1.8, −0.3, +0.5)% with the model B prior and from (+8.6, +2.5, +7.3, +3.1)% to (+4.6, +2.9, +1.8, −0.9)% with the flat toy, and over the plotted bins of Figure 3 from up to 29% to ≤ 6%. The Δ error bar then carries four terms — statistics, the shape fit, the response Monte Carlo (0.1–1.0% per plotted bin) and the spread over priors the shape family cannot absorb (1–8%) — of which the prior spread dominates in the best-measured bins, where the one-year statistics are ≲ 4%, and statistics elsewhere.

6 · The coherent channel

6.1 · Detecting the intact recoil

In coherent diffraction the recoil retains the beam's magnetic rigidity — A/Z = 2 exactly, and the diffractive momentum loss xP < 0.01 changes it by less than 1% — and emerges at θ = pT/p ≲ 1 mrad, inside the beam. 6.2 · Rate model

No diffractive prediction exists for a light nucleus (the lightest published case is Ca), so the f₀ band sits below HERA ep diffraction (10–15% of DIS [23]) and heavy-nucleus saturation estimates (20–25% [24]), a loosely bound A = 6 nucleus taking the smaller coherent share, and spans a factor 2 either side of 0.04; parameterizing fcoh in Bjorken x rather than xP is optimistic for high-mass diffraction, which the band is meant to cover. The scale xcoh = 0.01 follows the diffractive practice xP < 0.01 of §6.1 rather than geometry, and sits a factor two inside the geometric point: the coherence length lc = 0.105 fm/xP falls below the ⁶Li diameter (≈ 5 fm) only near xP ≈ 0.02, and the slope B = 50 GeV⁻² (band 40–60) follows from B = ⟨r²⟩/3 with the matter radius (the charge radius gives ≈ 57 [25]; gluonic distributions may be wider [26]), valid below the form-factor minimum at |t| ≈ 0.31 GeV² [27,28]. One year produces 1.2×10⁸ coherent events, of which the 0.20 GeV tag keeps 1.7×10⁷ (1.7×10⁸ in ten years); the best super-bin, x ∈ [0.0013, 0.0025], Q² ∈ [1, 1.7] GeV², holds 1.75×10⁶ and gives δÂ = 1.8×10⁻³ (one year) and 0.6×10⁻³ (ten years) on the amplitude per unit Pzz, i.e. 5σ floors at 0.9% and 0.3%; at Pzz = 0.6 the visible modulations are 0.6× these.

6.3 · The modulation and its two mechanisms

The cos 2φ′ modulation of the tagged yield carries two mechanisms. The deformation term is scaled from the polarized-deuteron colour-glass calculation of Mäntysaari et al. εB0 ≡ ΔB₀/B is scaled from the deuteron value of 0.21 by two indicators that disagree: the relative quadrupole deformation Q/⟨r²⟩ of ⁶Li (0.012) is about one fifth of the deuteron's (0.063) and opposite in sign, while the α–d asymptotic D/S ratio is disputed — a transfer measurement gives η = +0.0003(9), consistent with zero, older sub-Coulomb analyses favour η ≈ −0.01, and the ab initio no-core shell model with continuum gives −0.027, the deuteron's own 0.0256 in magnitude [31]. We adopt εB0 ∈ −(0.04–0.13) with default −0.08, leaving near-zero deformation open; the D/S indicator alone would allow up to the deuteron's −0.21, and the 5σ floors cover detection or exclusion anywhere in that range. The band's width is genuine theoretical uncertainty: even exact few-body calculations miss the ⁶Li quadrupole cancellation by a factor ≈ 3 [32], and the gluonic deformation need not share the cancellation of the charge sector. The sign flip relative to the deuteron is a testable prediction (Q(⁶Li) < 0), and tensor modulations of comparable size are established at these |t|: the elastic e-d T₂₀ = −(0.1–0.3) [33] and HERMES Azz(d) ≈ −1% at x ≈ 0.01 [34], the latter attributed to coherent double scattering [35–37]. The flat gluon-transversity term is separately bounded at 3×10⁻³–10⁻² (at Pzz = 1) from the bare lattice φ transversity [6] divided by the ~10× NPLQCD deuteron suppression [7], with the maximal Drell–Yan scenario [5] as the ceiling. ⟨a₂⟩ averages the m-state populations with equal weights; weighting them by their m-dependent rates lowers it by up to 27%, a one-sided shift inside the εB0 band.

The two mechanisms separate by shape: deformation is linear in t, sign-flipped relative to the deuteron and expected to weaken toward small xP (JIMWLK evolution washes it out [30]), while exotic glue is flat in t and persists at small x, where it connects to the elliptic-gluon programme [38]. In a two-component fit a₂(t) = cdef·|t| + ag per xP bin, the ten-year floor of 0.3% per unit Pzz lies at or below the flat term's band and an order of magnitude below the deformation term, so both components are expected to be resolved for the 0.20 GeV tag. Incoherent breakup is m-state-blind in this mechanism [10]: it dilutes the modulation but cannot mimic it. And deformation-driven modulations, the polarized-deuteron imaging signal, are bounded on ⁶Li by its quadrupole moment, Q = −0.0806(6) fm² ≈ Q(⁷Li)/50 [39]: a sizable flat-in-t cos 2φ on ⁶Li cannot be a shape effect, and the mechanisms entangled for the deuteron separate in the ⁶Li/d comparison — the null test unavailable to the deuteron.

That the analysis closes once a tag exists has been demonstrated with the reconstructed-level chain [16]: the recoil is transported to the pots with divergence smearing and an aperture, the spin-state ratio is formed in the two azimuths and fitted with an acceptance-weighted template basis in which the pot geometry's own ⟨cos 2β⟩ cancels, and reflection-forbidden sin terms serve as null columns. 7 · Systematics and assumptions

Two extraction systematics survive the spin-state ratio: the polarimetry scale δPzz/Pzz ≤ 5% (the working value this programme specifies, Report 2 §6; 3% is the optimistic case, reached on stored deuterons at the Nuclotron and on the HERMES storage cell), a 1:1 normalisation on Δ̂ common with Azz and with tensor polarimetry still requiring dedicated development (Lamb-shift or Breit–Rabi-type polarimeters) [8], and radiative corrections for tensor observables, which have not been calculated. The unpolarized part is now bounded rather than assumed: collinear initial-state radiation cannot fake a cos 2φ′ — the covariant azimuth is invariant under k → (1 − z)k for a massless target — it is common to the two fills and cancels in the ratio, and it leaves x = Q²e/(s yΣ) exact, moving only the Q²e label of the bin by 1/(1 − z); the migration that label carries biases Δ̂ by +0.5 to +1.2% at the four sweet spots on the 25% Gaussian hadronic-y stand-in and +0.4 to +0.9% through the PYTHIA final state with the calibrated Σ scale (≤2.9% once the generator window is opened below Q² = 0.15 GeV² and the low-Q² feed-in saturates, ≤0.25% behind a HERA-style E − pz window the chain holds in reserve rather than applies), against the ≤5% gate this programme set for its simulation letter (Report 2 §7). The tensor-sector correction is a different object and no unpolarized study stands in for it; the unpolarized QED tail is calculable and doubles as calibration. The detector nuisances have been measured rather than argued, holding the response fixed and varying one thing with common random numbers: a ±1% electron energy scale moves Δ̂ by 0.3–1.0% at the four bins, a hadronic-resolution mismatch (0.30 generated, 0.25 corrected) by 0.5–1.4%, a 1% residual hadronic-scale error by 0.1–0.6%, all above the 0.06–0.28% Monte Carlo floor and below the bin-centering model dependence of §5.3, while an η tilt of the electron-identification efficiency (≤ 0.03%) and the Yellow Report's η-dependent calorimeter table (≤ 0.1%) are unresolved at that floor. In interpretation, a ΔΔ-isobar admixture can mimic Δ at leading twist [12] and conventional double scattering generates the low-x b₁ [35–37]; the Q² lever arm and the ⁶Li/d comparison discriminate against both. For the coherent channel, an error in the unpolarized cos 2(α − β) harmonic u₂ reaches the flat term at first order through at δu₂ ⟨cos 2β⟩ — a ZEUS-1σ δu₂ [43] moves ae by 3–9% at the tagging optics of §6.1 at 5 × 40.8 — 20% there under the slot geometry assumed before the aperture was measured — and 4–13% at 18 × 137.5, and fitting u₁ and u₂ from the spin-averaged counts against the acceptance instead of taking them from the prior leaves 0.2–6.3% (Report 2 Table 6) — and a fill-to-fill change of the pot cutout enters through the acceptance's own ⟨cos 2β⟩: at the tagging cutout a 10⁻³ change of the horizontal half-width moves at by 0.5–9.1% and a 10⁻² one by 3.5–83.3% over the seven |t| bins at 5 × 40.8 (0.2–31.2% and 2.4–298.6% over the three configurations), the sensitivity climbing steeply towards the lowest bin, where the horizontal edge cuts a steep part of the spectrum, and where the earlier slot geometry amplified 10⁻³ to 19% in every bin [16]. The pots' envelope must therefore be stable to 10⁻³ between the spin states — which holds the four bins above 0.05 GeV² to 1.9% or better, the three below it needing 10⁻⁴, which leaves them within 0.9% at 5 × 40.8 and 3.8% across the three configurations — or the states must alternate bunch by bunch. 8 · Summary and outlook

A transversely tensor-polarized ⁶Li beam at the EIC measures Δ(x,Q²) in inclusive DIS at 21–43σ per (x, Q²) bin in one year under the moment-constrained scenario, and at 3–11σ per bin for a flat amplitude of 3×10⁻³ per unit Pzz even at Pzz = 0.3, discriminating between interpretations of the bag moment by the x dependence and extracting xΔ(x,Q²) to 2–9% in the best bins. The result survives reconstruction with the spin-state ratio and a hadronic-method y, at the price of bin purities that the calorimeter noise floor controls. The measurement makes three demands on the machine that are specific and answerable: tensor polarization delivered as alternating m = ±1-rich and m = 0-rich bunches, transverse at the interaction point; a cos 2φ′ acceptance harmonic stable to 10⁻⁴ between the spin states and a pot envelope stable to 10⁻³ — 10⁻⁴ in the three |t| bins below 0.05 GeV² (§7) — or the bunch-by-bunch alternation that makes any relative-luminosity error a constant; and a tensor polarimetry scale at the few-per-cent level.

The coherent channel carries the same physics with a null test the deuteron cannot offer. It is also a requirement rather than a projection: the intact ⁶Li cannot be tagged at IP6 with any published optics, at any energy, and what recovers it is the tagging optics of Figure 4 or the secondary focus of the second interaction region. The tagging optics we propose (§6.1) — 18 × 137.5 GeV/u with the horizontal β* raised ≈ 89× and pots at 0.12 mrad, or 5 × 40.8 GeV/u at ≈ 46× and 0.36 mrad, the vertical plane untouched, as a separate running configuration — recovers a 33–37% tag at 1/9.5–1/6.8 of the luminosity: a 9–11σ measurement per year of the shape term with its predicted sign flip against the deuteron, and 5σ on a 1% exotic-glue term in three years — 2.6 at the top configuration, 3.0 at the low one — both on the counting floor of the best (x, Q²) super-bin alone, while the seven-|t|-bin fit of §6.3 over the whole tagged sample measures the flat term to δae = 0.00074 and 0.00121 per year; a second interaction region's secondary focus would deliver 4–6× more at the same luminosity. The highest-leverage inputs from outside the programme are, on the machine side, a light-ion optics with β* chosen for tagging and a charge-identification study of the pot readout; on the theory side, a rerun of the IP-Glasma polarized-deuteron setup with an α–d cluster density, which would replace both εB0 indicators and the f₀ band at once, a lattice Δ moment for a light nucleus, and radiative corrections for tensor observables.
