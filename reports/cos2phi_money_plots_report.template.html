Nuclear gluonometry with a tensor-polarized ⁶Li beam at the Electron-Ion Collider: projected inclusive and coherent cos 2φ measurements

C. Peng

Physics Division, Argonne National Laboratory

Draft for circulation · 2026-08-27, numbers restated 2026-08-28 (first version 2026-08-10; revision history in Appendix A) · companions: Report 0 (physics case), Report 2 (reconstruction chain), Report 3 (machine and detector)

Abstract. The double-helicity-flip structure function Δ(x,Q²) of a spin-1 target is generated at leading twist by gluons alone and has never been measured. We project its measurement at the Electron-Ion Collider with a transversely tensor-polarized ⁶Li beam, in inclusive deep inelastic scattering, where only the scattered electron is detected, and in coherent diffraction with the ⁶Li recoiling intact. With the leading-order bag-model moment of Sather and Schmidt ported to ⁶Li as a labelled scenario, the inclusive cos 2φ amplitudes at the four most sensitive (x, Q²) bins, x = 0.011–0.14 and Q² = 1.1–14 GeV², are (0.44–0.95)×10⁻² at Pzz = 0.6, against single-state truth-level statistical errors of (1.4–4.5)×10⁻⁴ in one EIC year (10 fb⁻¹ per nucleon): a 21–44σ measurement per bin whose x dependence discriminates between moment-constrained interpretations, and an extraction of xΔ(x,Q²) itself to 2–9% in the best bins. At the reconstructed level, with the spin-state-sorted ratio of m = ±1-rich and m = 0-rich bunches and a hadronic-method y from a PYTHIA 8 final state, the errors are (0.9–3.0)×10⁻⁴ and the fit closes on the response-folded truth. The coherent channel carries the same modulation with two separable mechanisms — a nuclear-shape term anchored on the polarized-deuteron colour-glass calculation, predicted to flip sign for ⁶Li, and a flat exotic-glue term — and ⁶Li's anomalously small quadrupole moment makes it a null test. Its intact recoil keeps the beam rigidity and, with the Yellow Report's beam divergences, cannot be tagged at IP6 with any published optics; the channel defines a far-forward requirement — a dedicated tagging optics or the second interaction region's secondary focus — and for a reference tag with a 0.20 GeV near-beam envelope at the high-acceptance luminosity — which no IP6 optics provides — one year would yield 1.7×10⁷ events and a 5σ sensitivity to an amplitude of 0.9% per unit Pzz, against a shape term of 6% and an exotic-glue term of 0.3–1% on the same footing. A tagging optics at IP6 — the horizontal β* raised 50–180× with the pots following the envelope, the vertical plane untouched — recovers a 32–42% tag at 1/7–1/13 of the luminosity: 2.6–6.1×10⁶ tagged events per year, an 8–11σ measurement of the shape term per year and 5σ on a 1% exotic-glue term in 3–4 years, 3–8× below what a second interaction region's secondary focus would deliver at the same luminosity.

1 · Introduction

A spin-1 target can absorb two units of photon helicity in the forward Compton amplitude. The structure function that measures this, Δ(x,Q²), receives no leading-twist contribution from quarks or from bound spin-½ nucleons, so in a nucleus it is direct evidence for glue that is not inherited from the individual nucleons — "exotic glue" in the language of Jaffe and Manohar's nuclear gluonometry [1,2]. It has never been measured for any target; the one prior experimental document is a JLab letter of intent for a ¹⁴N target, without a sensitivity projection [3]. The only absolute normalizations available are moment estimates: a bag-model calculation for the spin-3/2 Δ⁺⁺ baryon [4], maximal-transversity scenarios for proton–deuteron Drell–Yan [5], and lattice-QCD matrix elements for the φ meson [6] and the deuteron [7]. The EPIOS white paper on polarized ion beams at the EIC names spin-1 ⁶Li among the nuclei where a discovery is most likely [8], and the companion physics case [9] argues why ⁶Li — a polarized deuteron bound to an inert α particle, with A/Z = 2 and deuteron-like spin dynamics in the ring — is the practical choice.

This paper presents the first numerical projection of a Δ measurement for any target. Two channels are considered. In inclusive DIS on a transversely tensor-polarized ⁶Li beam the scattered electron's azimuth about the alignment axis acquires a cos 2φ modulation proportional to Δ. In coherent diffraction, e ⁶Li → e′ X ⁶Li(g.s.), the nucleus stays in its ground state and the modulation of the tagged yield mixes the gluonic term with a nuclear-shape term that the polarized-deuteron calculation of Mäntysaari et al. [10] predicts — and ⁶Li, with a quadrupole moment fifty times smaller than ⁷Li's, separates the two. Section 2 states the observable, Section 3 the simulation framework, Section 4 the Δ model, Section 5 the inclusive projections at truth and reconstructed level, Section 6 the coherent channel with the far-forward requirement it imposes, Section 7 the systematics and assumptions.

Three properties of this relation shape the analysis. First, with F₂ = 2x(1 + R)F₁ the inversion becomes Δ/F₁ = −Â[2(1 + R) + y²/(1 − y)], so at the y ≲ 0.03 of the sensitive region F₂ cancels and R = σL/σT is the only structure-function input that reaches Δ/F₁. Second, the amplitude is exactly linear in Δ (§5.3). Third, the target-mass corrections are small: at the four bins of §3.4, γ² = 4M²x²/Q² ≤ 0.006, so the approximation set of the simulation — A∥ = D·g₁/F₁ with the γ²-suppressed term dropped, g₂ = g₂WW, no target-mass terms — is adequate. One finite-γ effect is physical rather than a labelling matter: the tensor-polarized b₁–b₃ terms feed the cos 2φ amplitude at O(γ²), bounded by 1.15 γ² b₁/F₁ (the full Cosyn et al. Eq. 17e combination, ≈ 6.9× the γ²b₁/6 of the b₁ term alone) [11]; for a HERMES-like b₁ this is ≤ 1% of the projected amplitudes at the four bins and is computable from the same data's Azz.

The size of Δ is unknown to within an order of magnitude. The bag-model moment of Sather and Schmidt, ∫x Δ dx = −0.012 αs with its Q² evolution but no x dependence [4], puts the asymmetry at the per-mille level; the explicit αs(Q²) is the leading-order Wilson coefficient of the photon–gluon box, so the O(αs) suppression relative to F₁ is model-independent while −0.012 is the bag estimate. Lattice QCD adds two results, both at unphysical pion masses: the bare φ-meson matrix element A₂ = 0.23(2)(5) at mπ = 450 MeV [6], which converts to ∫x Δ|φ ≈ −0.006 αs (our conversion, unrenormalized), and the NPLQCD deuteron δG moment of −0.010(3), bare at mπ ≈ 806 MeV [7] — a transversity-to-momentum ratio roughly ten times below the φ's, implying the opposite sign for the deuteron's Δ moment. Percent-level asymmetries arise only in the maximal-transversity (ΔTg = Δg) Drell–Yan scenarios their authors call optimistic [5]. Nothing exists for ⁶Li or any A > 2 nucleus beyond a ΔΔ-isobar estimate for the deuteron [12], so the literature brackets the nuclear Δ in both directions: suppressed, as in the NPLQCD deuteron, or enhanced by binding in analogy with the EMC effect [8]. The projections therefore carry the moment-constrained model of §4 together with the scenario band Δ/F₁ ∈ 10⁻³–10⁻².

3 · Simulation framework

3.1 · Beams, polarization and luminosity

EIC ions are γ-matched to the proton rather than rigidity-scaled, because the two rings must share a revolution period, so the ⁶Li menu is 40.8 GeV/u and 99.5–137.5 GeV/u, the top value being the rigidity cap 275 GeV × Z/A [8,13]. The projections are made at the mid configuration, e(10 GeV) × ⁶Li(99.5 GeV/u), √sNN = 63 GeV, with the low (5 × 40.8) and top (18 × 137.5) configurations used where the hadronic reconstruction or the far-forward acceptance depends on the energy. The in-ring tensor polarization is a placeholder Pzz = 0.6 (the ANL source targets Pzz ≥ 0.8 [9]; transport to the ring is an open accelerator question, and every statistical error scales as 1/Pzz), the alignment axis is the vertical — the stable spin direction of the arcs, native at the interaction point — and the electrons are unpolarized. Luminosity is quoted per nucleon: 10 fb⁻¹/u, "one EIC year" in the Yellow Report's accounting (30 weeks at 10³³ cm⁻² s⁻¹ with 60% efficiency [13]), and 100 fb⁻¹/u for the ten-year programme, all of it in the transverse-tensor fill. No lithium luminosity has been published; the yields scale linearly with whatever the machine delivers.

3.2 · Generator and structure functions

No public generator produces polarized e+A events for A > 1: BeAGLE is unpolarized, DJANGOH and PEPSI are nucleon-level and longitudinal-only. The generator used here, polligen, implements the missing component — the spin-density matrix ⊗ structure-function master formula with spin-correlated sampling — and takes the hadronic final state from a PYTHIA 8 production of 8 M events over the three beam configurations. It is validated against the analytic asymmetry formulas and by pseudo-experiments, including unbiased cos 2φ recovery under φ-acceptance holes; a full-luminosity pseudo-experiment draws each φ-bin count from a Poisson distribution with the exact expected yield, which is statistically identical to event-level sampling.

Structure functions are toy parameterizations with PDF-grid backends (CT18NLO, EPPS21, NNPDFpol11) behind one interface, so rates hold to a factor ≈ 1.5 (the toy F₂ is within ±37% of CT18 over the acceptance) and asymmetry shapes are scenarios by construction. R = σL/σT is a placeholder of the right magnitude, 0.14–0.18 at the four bins of §3.4, in every projection below; the published SLAC/E143 R1998 world fit [14], all three of its functional forms, sits behind the same hook, and switching to it moves Δ/F₁ by +18, +19, +8 and −4% at the four bins, with a further 0.5–6% from the spread of the fit forms — a correction to apply rather than a band, and the systematic on every Δ quoted below until it is.

3.3 · Estimators

The figures use a single spin state: a binned least-squares fit of N(φ′) = C(1 + A cos 2φ′) rather than the 2⟨cos 2φ′⟩ moment, which realistic φ holes bias by more than the signal while the fit stays unbiased, with δA = √(2/N)/Pzz validated by pseudo-experiments and no cos φ′ term, since the m-symmetric tensor fills set it to zero by construction. The azimuth φ′ is the covariant one built from the four-vectors [15], equal to φe − φS for a massless target and within 5 mrad of it for ⁶Li, and the 25 mrad crossing angle changes only odd harmonics of the electron azimuth because the ePIC axis is the electron beam.

The measurement itself cannot use a single state: the fit then also measures the detector's own cos 2φ′ acceptance harmonic divided by Pzz, and a 3% harmonic aligned with the vertical spin axis fakes an amplitude of 0.05, five to eleven times the signal. The estimator is therefore the spin-state-sorted ratio of m = ±1-rich (Pzz = +0.6) and m = 0-rich (−1.2) bunches. It cancels any acceptance common to both states bin by bin, turns relative-luminosity errors into a constant, and gives, with N the total of both states, δÂ = 2√(2/N)/(P₊ − P₀) = 0.67× the single-state error if the m = 0-rich fill reaches |Pzz| = 1.2. The cancellation of the acceptance, however, holds only for an acceptance common to both states: for two fills the bias is (ε₂⁺ − ε₂⁰)/(P₊ − P₀) at any luminosity split, so a 10⁻³ difference of the cos 2φ′ harmonic fakes 5.6×10⁻⁴, 6–13% of the projected amplitudes and 2–6 one-year statistical errors. Bunch-by-bunch alternation, or 10⁻⁴ stability of the harmonic between fills, is thus a requirement of the measurement rather than a refinement; with it, a relative-luminosity error between the states enters only as a constant.

3.4 · Acceptance and binning

Rates use per-nucleon luminosity, F₂ᴬ/A, and scattered-electron cuts |η| ≤ 3.5, E′ ≥ 0.5 GeV, 0.01 ≤ y ≤ 0.95 and W² ≥ 10 GeV², which accept 5.2×10⁹ events in one year (Figure 1). The four displayed (x, Q²) super-bins are selected by statistical significance within Q² bands — an unconstrained ranking concentrates at the lowest Q², whereas the Q² lever arm provides the consistency check against power-suppressed contributions — and each merges 3×3 cells of the 40×30 analysis grid (3×2 for the two Q² = 1.14 GeV² bins, clipped at the grid's Q² = 1 GeV² edge). They sit at (x, Q²) = (0.028, 1.14), (0.011, 1.14), (0.071, 3.13) and (0.141, 14.3 GeV²), i.e. at y = 0.010, 0.026, 0.011 and 0.026: the amplitude grows as (1 − y)/y², so the sensitivity lives at the lowest y the detector can reconstruct, which is what makes the kinematic reconstruction of §5.2 the central detector question.

The factor 1/3 restricts the exotic glue to the polarized deuteron-like pair while the whole-nucleus Pzz remains a separate factor, so nothing is double counted; the cluster picture would give ≈ 0.81 instead, an upside of ×2.4 that is not taken. The constraint is imposed at the rate-weighted ⟨Q²⟩ = 4.47 GeV² of the accepted phase space, and because F₁ evolves the realized moment varies by a few per cent across the plotted Q² slices; αs comes from the CT18NLO table. Model B imposes the same constraint without the F₁ factor and yields amplitudes a factor 3–56 lower at the four bins (20–56 in the Q² = 1.14 GeV² bins, 3–8 at the higher Q²); the A-versus-B spread is the dominant ansatz systematic, and both curves appear on every plot. Porting a single-hadron bag moment to a per-nucleon nuclear structure function has no precedent, so normalization and x dependence are both scenario choices, bracketed by model B from below and possible nuclear enhancement from above.

5 · Inclusive projections

5.1 · Truth level

Figure 2 shows the φ′ pseudo-data in the four super-bins with one-year statistics and the extracted amplitude versus x along the Q² = 1.14 GeV² slice. The injected amplitudes are 7.4, 4.4, 9.5 and 9.5 ×10⁻³ against one-year errors of 1.7, 1.4, 2.8 and 4.5 ×10⁻⁴ — 44, 31, 34 and 21σ — and ten-year errors of 0.55, 0.45, 0.87 and 1.4 ×10⁻⁴; the fitted values agree with the injected ones within 1.5 standard errors. Under the moment constraint the measurement is not a null test: the model-A-versus-B spread is resolved by the x dependence of the amplitude, and a flat amplitude of 3×10⁻³ per unit Pzz would remain a 3–11σ effect per bin even at Pzz = 0.3. At ten-year precision the statistical errors sit below the polarimetry scale systematic (3% of an amplitude of 10⁻² is 3×10⁻⁴), so the ten-year measurement is systematics-limited.

Figure 3 unfolds the same pseudo-measurements to the structure function, xΔ(x,Q²) per nucleon at the three Q² slices of Figure 1, with independent one- and ten-year draws and the model bin-centering factor K = Δmodel(xc, Q²c)/Amodel, bin; the area under each curve is the ported bag moment (÷3 dilution). The best bins reach Δ = −0.135 ± 0.003 at (x, Q²) = (0.02, 1.14), −0.070 ± 0.002 at (0.05, 3.13) and −0.0047 ± 0.0004 at (0.32, 14.3) in one year, i.e. 2–9% relative, and three times better in ten.

Three conclusions follow. The fit closes on the response-folded truth within the precision of the test: the eight pulls against the reconstructed-bin truth lie within 2.2 standard errors while the acceptance harmonic and the luminosity offset are on, and the sin 2φ′ coefficients are consistent with zero. The errors are 0.59–0.69 of the single-state truth-level values, because the 1.5× gain of the m = 0-rich fill outweighs the reconstruction efficiency of 0.38–0.66 — presuming the source delivers m = 0-rich bunches at |Pzz| = 1.2 with the same purity as the ±1 fill at 0.6. And the real final state costs purity rather than statistics: only 80–92% of the hadronic E − pz sum is within the acceptance and above threshold at the four bins, 70–85% through the full response — the rest escapes forward beyond the calorimeters (7–17%, the target-fragmentation side of a W ≈ 6–10 GeV system) or lies below threshold — a 13–27% scale bias on yΣ that an analysis calibrates from its simulation at the reconstructed point. Calibrated, the purities are 0.56–0.75 and the reconstructed-bin amplitude within 6% of the true-bin value (Table 1); uncalibrated they would be 0.42–0.68 and 5–21% low, and a 1% residual scale error moves Â by 0.1–0.6% [16]. The noise floor decides the resolution below y ≈ 0.02 — at the mid configuration the Σ-method δy/y at y = 0.005 runs 0.22 → 0.33 → 0.54 → 1.01 for 0 → 25 → 50 → 100 MeV of calorimeter noise on a hadronic E − pz sum of 0.2–0.5 GeV, a regime no published ePIC study covers. Each configuration selects its own four bins: at 5 × 40.8 and 18 × 137.5 they resolve to 0.38 / 0.23 / 0.24 / 0.11 and 0.23 / 0.19 / 0.21 / 0.18, so the low-x bins improve with beam energy (the hadronic E − pz = 2Eey grows with it) while the (0.141, 14.3 GeV²) bin degrades, 0.11 → 0.15 → 0.18, and is best measured at the low configuration. Unfolded to Δ, the best reconstructed bins reach statistical errors δΔ = 2.5 (2.3) ×10⁻³ at Q² = 1.14 GeV² and 1.2 (1.1) ×10⁻³ at 3.13 GeV² in one year with the stand-in (PYTHIA) y, at purities 0.56–0.57 (0.44–0.50).

5.3 · Bin centering

Correcting the reconstructed amplitude to a point value of Δ with the model's own K is circular. Because the amplitude is exactly linear in Δ, the response can instead be applied as a linear operator and a Δ(x) shape fitted through it per Q² slice on a bounded grid. Correcting the same pseudo-data with either of two deliberately wrong priors, the model dependence of K at the four super-bins falls from (−4.1, +8.0, −5.5, +4.9)% to (−1.5, −1.8, −0.3, +0.5)% with the model B prior and from (+8.6, +2.5, +7.3, +3.2)% to (+4.6, +3.0, +1.8, −0.9)% with the flat toy, and over the plotted bins of Figure 3 from up to 29% to ≤ 6%. The Δ error bar then carries four terms — statistics, the shape fit, the response Monte Carlo (0.15–1.5% per bin) and the spread over priors the shape family cannot absorb (1–8%) — of which the prior spread dominates in the best-measured bins, where the one-year statistics are ≲ 4%, and statistics elsewhere.

6 · The coherent channel

6.1 · Detecting the intact recoil

In coherent diffraction the recoil retains the beam's magnetic rigidity — A/Z = 2 exactly, and the diffractive momentum loss xP < 0.01 changes it by less than 1% — and emerges at θ = pT/p ≲ 1 mrad, inside the beam. Of the far-forward system at IP6 — the B0 spectrometer, the off-momentum detectors, the Roman Pots and the zero-degree calorimeter, with the acceptance windows of Refs. [18–20] — only the pots can see such a recoil, and only where it clears both the 10σ beam envelope and the pots' own aperture. Both are constraints on the angle at the interaction point, so with an exponential t distribution of slope B (≈ 50 GeV⁻², §6.2) the tagged fraction is

The conclusion is unambiguous: with any published optics the coherent intact-⁶Li channel does not exist at IP6, at any energy. It exists under two conditions. A tagging optics, with β* raised until the envelope sits at pT = 1/√B, where luminosity times acceptance is maximal, gives εtag = 1/e at every configuration; the 1/e column of Table 2 is the circular-isotropic derivation, and priced for planar pots below the optimum is a horizontal-only de-squeeze, β*x ≈ 50× / 180× / 90× (≈ 45 m at 5 × 41 for a high-acceptance β*x of 0.9 m), within the range of the forward-physics optics demonstrated at the LHC — but the pots must then approach horizontally to 0.33, 0.17 and 0.12 mrad where the silicon now sits at 2.0 / 1.35 / 1.03, so the two levers are multiplicative and the second is the near-beam question of Report 4 [21]. Alternatively, the secondary focus of the second interaction region lets pots at z = 44.0/45.5 m reach pT ≈ 0, with published intact-recoil efficiencies of 47% (d), 32% (³He), 29% (⁴He) and 17.8% (⁷Li) at top energy, rising steeply at lower energies [22]; no ⁶Li case is published and our interpolation is ≈ 20%.

Pricing the tagging optics. The β* lever is a machine lattice setting, not a far-forward one — it is exactly what separates the Yellow Report's high-acceptance optics from its high-divergence optics — and it is paid for in luminosity: σθ ∝ 1/√β* while L ∝ 1/β* per plane, which the Yellow Report's own optics pairs reproduce to 5% (10.0 against 3.14×10³³ cm⁻²s⁻¹ at 275 GeV for a divergence ratio 119/65) [13]. Because a recoil escapes through the horizontal gap between the pots or through the vertical one, only the plane it escapes through needs the tagging β*: Figure 4 therefore de-squeezes the horizontal plane alone, holds the vertical at its high-acceptance value and pays L/LHA = 1/√r rather than 1/r, with the envelope treated as the planar pots see it — a rectangle 10σh × 10σv in angle, the dispersion at the pots (D ≈ 0.3 m with the Yellow Report Δp/p) added to the horizontal side — for pots either following the envelope or fixed at the aperture measured today. With the pots fixed, the acceptance stays below 10⁻⁶ at any β*: the far-forward side must move with the optics. With the pots following, the yield peaks at r = β*x/β*x,HA = 50, 180 and 90 at the three configurations, where ε = 0.42, 0.32 and 0.33, the horizontal envelope sits at 0.33, 0.17 and 0.12 mrad (0.08–0.10 GeV) and the luminosity at 1/7, 1/13 and 1/9.5 of the high-acceptance value; de-squeezing both planes, the naive reading of Table 2, would cost 1/24–1/70 for a fifth of the yield. Taking the placeholder of §3.1, 10 fb⁻¹/u per year, as the high-acceptance luminosity, the tagging optics yields 2.6×10⁶, 3.0×10⁶ and 6.1×10⁶ tagged events per year — 2.3×, 5.6× and 3.9× below the 0.20 GeV reference tag at that luminosity — and the best super-bin of §6.2 reaches 5σ floors of 1.7%, 2.1% and 1.6% per unit Pzz per year. In the optics' own window, ⟨|t|⟩ = 0.031–0.035 GeV², the shape term of §6.3 is 3.1–3.5% per unit Pzz — a 9σ, 8σ and 11σ measurement per year — and a 1% exotic-glue term reaches 5σ in 2.8, 4.4 and 2.6 years. The top configuration is the most productive at equal luminosity, with four times the coherent events of 5 × 41. If the second interaction region delivered the same luminosity as the high-acceptance optics, its secondary focus at our interpolated ≈ 20% would still be worth 3×, 8× and 6× the IP6 optimum in yield (Figure 4, dash-dotted line); the factor scales with LIR8/LHA, which is not published, and the published efficiencies rise toward lower energies [22]. Two assumptions carry these numbers and are stated as such: the electron β* must be raised in step to keep the spot sizes matched, and the far-forward transport of the de-squeezed lattice must remain parallel-to-point at the pots (R₁₁σ* ≪ R₁₂σθ, as in TOTEM's high-β* optics) for the envelope to be 10R₁₂σθ at all — true of the present lattice at the Yellow Report optics, and the first thing to establish for a de-squeezed one.

The reference tag of the projections. Figures 1 and 5 and §6.2 are drawn for a near-beam envelope of 0.20 GeV — εtag = 13.5% (9–20% for B ∈ 40–60 GeV⁻²), the documented limit of coherent ³He tagging at IP6 [22] — at the high-acceptance luminosity, which no IP6 optics provides: conservative in the tagged fraction against the 32–42% of the tagging optics and the ≈ 20% of IR-8, but 2.3×, 5.6× and 3.9× above what the tagging optics yields at the three configurations. They stand as the physics case for a tag, not as an IP6 projection.

The configuration we propose. A lithium tagging optics at 18 × 137.5 GeV/u with the horizontal β* raised ≈ 90× over the high-acceptance setting, the vertical plane untouched, and the Roman Pots inserted horizontally to ≈ 0.12 mrad (≈ 4 mm at the pot plane if the present lever of 30.6 m survived the de-squeeze; the de-squeezed transfer matrix is one of the inputs requested below) — or, at the lowest energy, 5 × 40.8 GeV/u with β*x ≈ 50× (≈ 45 m for a high-acceptance β*x of 0.9 m) and pots at 0.33 mrad — run as a separate running configuration, as the Yellow Report foresees its high-acceptance optics, for a share of the year with which the yields above scale (Table 4, row 8), while the inclusive measurement takes the high-divergence optics. It is a change to the interaction-region lattice, on the electron side as well, and to the pot insertion; whether a horizontal de-squeeze of that size is compatible with the crab cavities and keeps the far-forward transport parallel-to-point is part of what is asked. From the machine it needs the lithium divergence and the high-acceptance β* per configuration and the far-forward transfer matrix at the pot planes for the de-squeezed lattice; from the detector, the pot approach and the charge identification below; and its cost — a factor 7–13 in luminosity for the duration of the store — is the number to weigh against a second interaction region. Whichever provides the tag, one degeneracy remains: a Roman-Pot hit measures A/Z, and an intact ⁶Li, an α and a d at the same velocity share trajectory and timing. The second-detector design answers it with a Z²-scaling Cherenkov counter behind the pots [19]; for IP6 no charge-discrimination study exists, though the AC-LGAD readout digitizes an 8-bit charge over four planes and the α + d pair separates by hit topology [21].

6.2 · Rate model

No diffractive prediction exists for a light nucleus (the lightest published case is Ca), so the f₀ band brackets HERA ep diffraction (10–15% of DIS [23]) and heavy-nucleus saturation estimates (20–25% [24]); parameterizing fcoh in Bjorken x rather than xP is optimistic for high-mass diffraction, which the band is meant to cover. The scale xcoh = 0.01 marks where the coherence length lc = 0.105 fm/xP falls below the ⁶Li size, and the slope B = 50 GeV⁻² (band 40–60) follows from B = ⟨r²⟩/3 with the matter radius (the charge radius gives ≈ 57 [25]; gluonic distributions may be wider [26]), valid below the form-factor minimum at |t| ≈ 0.31 GeV² [27,28]. One year produces 1.2×10⁸ coherent events, of which the 0.20 GeV tag keeps 1.7×10⁷ (1.7×10⁸ in ten years); the best super-bin, x ∈ [0.0013, 0.0025], Q² ∈ [1, 1.7] GeV², holds 1.75×10⁶ and gives δÂ = 1.8×10⁻³ (one year) and 0.6×10⁻³ (ten years) on the amplitude per unit Pzz, i.e. 5σ floors at 0.9% and 0.3%; at Pzz = 0.6 the visible modulations are 0.6× these.

6.3 · The modulation and its two mechanisms

The cos 2φ′ modulation of the tagged yield carries two mechanisms. The deformation term is scaled from the polarized-deuteron colour-glass calculation of Mäntysaari et al. [10], the only published calculation of polarization-dependent azimuthal coefficients in coherent diffraction on any polarized nucleus (all forward citations checked; the group's subsequent work is unpolarized [29,30]). Their coefficients show a₂ growing linearly in |t| with a₂(m = 0) = −2a₂(m = ±1), from the 6% D-wave modulating the diffractive slope. Two conventions matter. The anchor's Eq. (9) expands d²σ/dΦd|t| ∝ 1 + 2ΣnaneinΦ with Φ the azimuth of the recoil relative to the polarization axis in the γ*d frame, so the cos 2Φ coefficient is 2a₂: the ⟨a₂⟩ quoted here — 0.036 at Pzz = 0.6, i.e. 0.060 per unit Pzz, the footing of the 5σ floors — and injected in Figure 5(d) understates the deformation modulation by a factor two, and the coherent numbers of this paper are conservative by ×2. And the modulation is one of the recoil azimuth φt − φS, not of the electron azimuth used as the axis of Figure 5(d); the reconstructed-level analysis fits both azimuths [16].

εB0 ≡ ΔB₀/B is scaled from the deuteron value of 0.21 by two indicators that disagree: the relative quadrupole deformation Q/⟨r²⟩ of ⁶Li (0.012) is about one fifth of the deuteron's (0.063) and opposite in sign, while the α–d asymptotic D/S ratio is disputed — a transfer measurement gives η = +0.0003(9), consistent with zero, older sub-Coulomb analyses favour η ≈ −0.01, and the ab initio no-core shell model with continuum gives −0.027, the deuteron's own 0.0256 in magnitude [31]. We adopt εB0 ∈ −(0.04–0.13) with default −0.08, leaving near-zero deformation open; the D/S indicator alone would allow up to the deuteron's −0.21, and the 5σ floors cover detection or exclusion anywhere in that range. The band's width is genuine theoretical uncertainty: even exact few-body calculations miss the ⁶Li quadrupole cancellation by a factor ≈ 3 [32], and the gluonic deformation need not share the cancellation of the charge sector. The sign flip relative to the deuteron is a testable prediction (Q(⁶Li) < 0), and tensor modulations of comparable size are established at these |t|: the elastic e-d T₂₀ = −(0.1–0.3) [33] and HERMES Azz(d) ≈ −1% at x ≈ 0.01 [34], the latter attributed to coherent double scattering [35–37]. The flat gluon-transversity term is separately bounded at 3×10⁻³–10⁻² (at Pzz = 1) from the bare lattice φ transversity [6] divided by the ~10× NPLQCD deuteron suppression [7], with the maximal Drell–Yan scenario [5] as the ceiling. ⟨a₂⟩ averages the m-state populations with equal weights; weighting them by their m-dependent rates lowers it by up to 27%, a one-sided shift inside the εB0 band.

The two mechanisms separate by shape: deformation is linear in t, sign-flipped relative to the deuteron and expected to weaken toward small xP (JIMWLK evolution washes it out [30]), while exotic glue is flat in t and persists at small x, where it connects to the elliptic-gluon programme [38]. In a two-component fit a₂(t) = cdef·|t| + ag per xP bin, the ten-year floor of 0.3% per unit Pzz lies at or below the flat term's band and an order of magnitude below the deformation term, so both components are expected to be resolved for the 0.20 GeV tag. Incoherent breakup is m-state-blind in this mechanism [10]: it dilutes the modulation but cannot mimic it. And deformation-driven modulations, the polarized-deuteron imaging signal, are bounded on ⁶Li by its quadrupole moment, Q = −0.0806(6) fm² ≈ Q(⁷Li)/50 [39]: a sizable flat-in-t cos 2φ on ⁶Li cannot be a shape effect, and the mechanisms entangled for the deuteron separate in the ⁶Li/d comparison — the null test unavailable to the deuteron.

That the analysis closes once a tag exists has been demonstrated with the reconstructed-level chain [16]: the recoil is transported to the pots with divergence smearing and an aperture, the spin-state ratio is formed in the two azimuths and fitted with an acceptance-weighted template basis in which the pot geometry's own ⟨cos 2β⟩ cancels, and reflection-forbidden sin terms serve as null columns. For a 0.73 mrad horizontal approach at the 5 × 41 configuration, the recoil smeared with a 73 µrad divergence (the approach being its 10σ envelope, close to the tagging-optics case of Table 2) — acceptance 7.8%, 1.4×10⁶ tagged events in the lowest of four |t| bins in one year — the fit recovers the deformation coefficient at = 0.115 ± 0.003, 0.181 ± 0.004 and 0.260 ± 0.009 against 0.119, 0.179 and 0.257 injected in the three bins that carry the statistics (the fourth, with 10⁴ events, returns 0.41 ± 0.02 against 0.354), resolves a pot roll to 6–10 mrad through the null columns, and recovers the flat term to ± 0.0014 in the lowest bin, where two independent pseudo-experiments return 0.0102 and 0.0151 against 0.010 injected — a residual bias of the template basis at this strongly anisotropic aperture, of the order of the one-year error, that a ten-year measurement would have to remove.

7 · Systematics and assumptions

Two extraction systematics survive the spin-state ratio: the polarimetry scale δPzz/Pzz ≈ 3%, common with Azz and with tensor polarimetry still requiring dedicated development (Lamb-shift or Breit–Rabi-type polarimeters) [8], and radiative corrections for tensor observables, which have not been calculated; the unpolarized QED tail is calculable and doubles as calibration. The detector nuisances have been measured rather than argued, holding the response fixed and varying one thing with common random numbers: a ±1% electron energy scale moves Δ̂ by 0.3–0.9% at the four bins, a hadronic-resolution mismatch (0.30 generated, 0.25 corrected) by 0.5–1.4%, a 1% residual hadronic-scale error by 0.1–0.6%, all above the 0.06–0.28% Monte Carlo floor and below the bin-centering model dependence of §5.3, while an η tilt of the electron-identification efficiency (≤ 0.03%) and the Yellow Report's η-dependent calorimeter table (≤ 0.1%) are unresolved at that floor. In interpretation, a ΔΔ-isobar admixture can mimic Δ at leading twist [12] and conventional double scattering generates the low-x b₁ [35–37]; the Q² lever arm and the ⁶Li/d comparison discriminate against both. For the coherent channel, an error in the unpolarized cos 2(α − β) harmonic u₂ reaches the flat term at first order through at δu₂ ⟨cos 2β⟩ — a ZEUS-1σ δu₂ [43] moves ae by 7–9% at the tagging optics of §6.1 (it was 20% under the slot geometry assumed before the aperture was measured) — and a fill-to-fill change of the pot cutout enters through the acceptance's own ⟨cos 2β⟩: at the tagging cutout a 10⁻³ change of the horizontal half-width moves at by 0.1–0.8% and a 10⁻² one by 3–8%, where the earlier slot geometry amplified 10⁻³ to 19% [16]. The pots' envelope must therefore be stable to 10⁻³ between the spin states, or the states must alternate bunch by bunch. Table 4 lists the assumptions and what a wrong one costs.

8 · Summary and outlook

A transversely tensor-polarized ⁶Li beam at the EIC measures Δ(x,Q²) in inclusive DIS at 21–44σ per (x, Q²) bin in one year under the moment-constrained scenario, and at 3–11σ per bin for a flat amplitude of 3×10⁻³ per unit Pzz even at Pzz = 0.3, discriminating between interpretations of the bag moment by the x dependence and extracting xΔ(x,Q²) to 2–9% in the best bins. The result survives reconstruction with the spin-state ratio and a hadronic-method y, at the price of bin purities that the calorimeter noise floor controls. The measurement makes three demands on the machine that are specific and answerable: tensor polarization delivered as alternating m = ±1-rich and m = 0-rich bunches, transverse at the interaction point; a cos 2φ′ acceptance and a pot envelope stable to 10⁻⁴ between the spin states, or the bunch-by-bunch alternation that makes any relative-luminosity error a constant; and a tensor polarimetry scale at the few-per-cent level.

The coherent channel carries the same physics with a null test the deuteron cannot offer. It is also a requirement rather than a projection: the intact ⁶Li cannot be tagged at IP6 with any published optics, at any energy, and what recovers it is the tagging optics of Figure 4 or the secondary focus of the second interaction region. The tagging optics we propose (§6.1) — 18 × 137.5 GeV/u with the horizontal β* raised ≈ 90× and pots at 0.12 mrad, or 5 × 40.8 GeV/u at ≈ 50× and 0.33 mrad, the vertical plane untouched, as a separate running configuration — recovers a 33–42% tag at 1/9.5–1/7 of the luminosity: a 9–11σ measurement per year of the shape term with its predicted sign flip against the deuteron, and 5σ on a 1% exotic-glue term in three years; a second interaction region's secondary focus would deliver 3–6× more at the same luminosity. The highest-leverage inputs from outside the programme are, on the machine side, a light-ion optics with β* chosen for tagging and a charge-identification study of the pot readout; on the theory side, a rerun of the IP-Glasma polarized-deuteron setup with an α–d cluster density, which would replace both εB0 indicators and the f₀ band at once, a lattice Δ moment for a light nucleus, and radiative corrections for tensor observables.
